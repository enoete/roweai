Getting started

Every prompt on this page works in both assistants. Here's where to find them.

Ask about your mailbox

Outlook
Click the Copilot icon in the toolbar to open the chat pane.
Gmail
Click the Gemini star at the top right to open the side panel.

Summarize one email

Outlook
Open the email and click Summary at the top.
Gmail
Open the email and click Summarize this email.

Get help replying

Outlook
Click Reply, then Draft with Copilot.
Gmail
Check the suggested replies, or use Help me write.
  • The assistant only sees your own mailbox, and it can miss things. Treat its answers as a starting point.
  • Nothing is sent until you click Send. You are responsible for every email that leaves your mailbox.
  • Be specific: say who, what time period, and what format you want back.
Step 1

Catch up: what needs my attention?

Start your day here. These prompts turn a full inbox into a short list.

Ask

Which emails from the past 24 hours need a reply from me? Give me the sender, subject, and what they need.

Summarize what I missed since [Friday afternoon]. Skip newsletters and automated notifications.

Which emails from clients need my attention today?

Is anyone waiting on a confirmation or an answer from me this week?

Follow up with

Rank those by urgency. Put anything with a deadline at the top.

Which of these can I just acknowledge with a quick reply?

Why did you include the email from [sender]?

Why didn't you include the email about [subject]?

Did you check the whole period? Show me anything older you may have skipped.

Someone else replying in the thread does not count as my reply. Check again.

Step 2

Understand a long thread

Open the email first, then ask. This saves reading 15 replies to find the one line that matters.

Ask

Summarize this thread in three bullets: what's decided, what's still open, and what's needed from me.

What exactly is [sender] asking me to do? Quote the sentence.

List every date, deadline, and amount mentioned in this thread.

Who is involved in this thread and what does each person want?

Follow up with

What has changed since my last reply?

Is any question in this thread still unanswered?

Explain [term] in plain language.

Step 3

Reply in your own voice

Open the email you are replying to, then ask.

!
Always end a drafting prompt with “Create it as a reply draft in Outlook” or “Create it as a reply draft in Gmail”. Without that line, the draft stays as text in the chat pane and no compose window opens.

Ask

Draft a short reply confirming [what you're confirming]. Keep it warm and natural, in my usual tone. No more than three sentences. Create it as a reply draft in [Outlook / Gmail].

Draft a quick acknowledgement: I received this and will respond by [day]. Create it as a reply draft in [Outlook / Gmail].

Draft a polite reply declining [request] and suggesting [alternative]. Create it as a reply draft in [Outlook / Gmail].

Draft a reply asking [sender] for [the missing document or information]. Create it as a reply draft in [Outlook / Gmail].

Follow up with

Make it shorter.

Make it warmer.

Make it more formal.

Remove anything that sounds like it was written by AI.

Sign off the way I usually do.

Add that I'll follow up by [Thursday].

The draft then opens as a reply in the thread, ready to review and send. In Gmail, also check the suggested replies under the email first; they're often good enough for a simple acknowledgement.

Step 4

Find things

Describe what you remember in plain words. You don't need exact subject lines or search operators.

Ask

Find the email where [person] sent the [document] last month.

When did I last hear from [person], and what was it about?

Show me emails with attachments from [company] this month.

What did we agree with [person] about [topic]?

Follow up with

Give me a link to the original email.

Has there been anything else from them since?

Step 5

Plan: deadlines, meetings, and follow-ups

Use these to make sure nothing slips, including replies you are still waiting for from others.

Ask

What deadlines or dates are mentioned in my emails this week?

Which emails did I send in the past week that haven't had a reply yet?

Prepare me for my [2 pm] meeting with [person]: the relevant emails and any open questions.

Draft a polite follow-up to [person] about [subject], since I haven't heard back. Create it as a reply draft in [Outlook / Gmail].

Follow up with

Turn these into a to-do list.

Add this to my tasks.

Copilot adds tasks to Microsoft To Do; Gemini adds them to Google Tasks.

Good habits and safety

The assistant is a fast helper, not a lawyer or a final reviewer.

  • Read every draft before sending. Check names, dates, amounts, and anything legal against the original email.
  • Don't rely on it for legal judgments. Use it for admin, summaries, and routine replies.
  • Keep client information inside the firm's tools. Use only Copilot in Outlook and Gemini in Gmail. Never paste client emails into outside AI apps such as ChatGPT.
  • Be suspicious of short lists. If an answer seems incomplete, ask: “Did you check everything?”
  • Report anything odd to IT, such as wrong summaries, invented details, or replies that don't sound like you.
